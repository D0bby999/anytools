---
title: Time card calculator — Frequently asked questions
---

## What does it total?

Hours worked per day from clock-in and clock-out times, summed across the week, then split into regular and overtime hours with pay for each.

## How is overtime decided?

Anything beyond 40 hours in the week is overtime, paid at 1.5 times the base rate. That is the common US weekly standard and it is what this tool applies.

## Does it handle daily overtime rules?

No. Some jurisdictions require overtime after a set number of hours in a single day regardless of the weekly total, and some have double-time thresholds. This calculator only applies the 40-hour weekly rule, so it will understate what you are owed where a daily rule applies.

## What time format should I use?

Twenty-four hour, as `HH:MM` — so 5pm is `17:00`. Entering a time that cannot be parsed contributes zero hours for that day rather than a wrong number.

## How do I enter a shift that crosses midnight?

Carefully — the day calculation works from clock-in to clock-out within a row, so a shift ending after midnight needs splitting across two rows, or the day will come out short or negative. This is the tool's sharpest edge; check any overnight shift by hand.

## Are unpaid breaks deducted?

Only if you exclude them yourself, by entering the worked segments rather than one span from arrival to departure. The tool has no break field, so a single row from `09:00` to `17:30` counts the whole 8.5 hours including any lunch.

## Is this before or after tax?

Before. It multiplies hours by rates and nothing else — no tax, no deductions, no benefits. Your take-home will be lower.

## Can I use this to check my paycheck?

As a sanity check, yes, and a mismatch is worth investigating. But it is not authoritative: your employer may apply daily overtime, different rounding of clock times, or contractual terms this does not know about. Take a real discrepancy to your payroll department or a labour adviser rather than to a calculator.

## Is my timesheet data stored?

No. It stays in your browser and nothing is transmitted.
