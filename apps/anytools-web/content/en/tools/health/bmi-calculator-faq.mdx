title: BMI calculator — Frequently asked questions
---

## What exactly does this calculator compute?

Body Mass Index: your weight in kilograms divided by the square of your height in metres. Nothing else goes into it — not age, not sex, not body composition. If you enter pounds and feet, the tool converts to metric first and runs the same formula.

## Which category boundaries does it use?

The World Health Organization adult cut-offs: under 18.5 is underweight, 18.5–24.9 is the normal range, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 and above is obesity, split into class I (30–34.9), class II (35–39.9) and class III (40+). These are the thresholds this tool applies.

## Why can a healthy, muscular person get an "overweight" BMI?

Because BMI has no way to tell muscle from fat. It only knows your total mass. Muscle is denser than fat, so a lifter or a rugby player at a low body fat percentage can land in the overweight or even obese band. The number is not wrong — it is measuring the wrong thing for that person. If you want a composition estimate instead, use the [body fat calculator](/en/health/body-fat-calculator).

## Do the same cut-offs apply to everyone?

No. The WHO has noted that the relationship between BMI and health risk differs across populations, and several health authorities in Asia use lower action points — commonly around 23 for increased risk and 27.5 for high risk — because cardiometabolic risk appears at a lower BMI. This tool reports the standard international bands; if you are using it against local clinical guidance, check which thresholds that guidance uses.

## Is BMI valid for children or during pregnancy?

Not as calculated here. Children and teenagers are assessed against age- and sex-specific percentile charts rather than fixed adult cut-offs, and BMI is not a meaningful measure during pregnancy. This calculator implements the adult formula only.

## What is BMI actually good for, then?

Population-level screening and tracking your own trend over time. It is cheap, needs two numbers, and is reproducible — which is why clinicians and epidemiologists still use it as a first filter. It is a starting point for a conversation, not a diagnosis.

## Does my data go anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in your browser as you type. Nothing is sent to a server, stored, or logged.

## Should I make a health decision based on this number?

No. This is an estimation tool. Weight, body composition and health risk are clinical questions that depend on your history, bloodwork and circumstances — talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian before acting on any number here.
